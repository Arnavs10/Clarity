What happens to your document

Nothing is stored. Your contract is read in memory, audited clause by clause, and discarded once the result is returned. It is not written to disk, not saved to a database, and not retained after you close the page. There is no account, no sign in, and no history.

An offer letter or a lease carries your name, your salary or your address. A tool that asks you to hand that over and keeps it has taken more than it needed to answer your question.

Where the reasoning comes from

Some flags rest on an Act of Parliament, and those quote the section directly. Some rest on a regulator's direction, which binds the companies it covers. Some rest on official guidance that is not binding, and Clarity says so rather than implying otherwise. And some rest on nothing more than how unusual a term is raised as a judgement about the drafting, which is said plainly as a judgement and never dressed up as law.

Wherever a flag rests on a written source, that source is shown to you. If it cannot be shown, the flag is withheld rather than displayed. A flag you cannot check is a flag you should not trust.

What it will not do

It is not a lawyer and does not replace one. It reads the clause types that most commonly cause problems, which is not every clause type that exists. Nothing flagged means nothing matched, not that a document is safe. And it never sends anything on your behalf: the negotiation message is a draft for you to read, edit and send yourself.
